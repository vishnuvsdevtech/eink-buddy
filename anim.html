What the research says

The 2.9" module is an SSD1680-class panel and the V2 spec explicitly lists support partial refresh. So the limit isn't capability, it's time: a full refresh takes ~2.4 s with a visible invert cycle, a partial is ~0.3–0.6 s with no flash, and a "fast" waveform sits in between at the cost of contrast. Firmware in the wild escalates PARTIAL → FAST → FULL as ghosting accumulates, because a partial never fully settles the pigment.

The number that kills the idea

A partial refresh is ~0.4 s at ~15 mA. At 3 fps that's a duty cycle above 100 % — the panel is always refreshing, averaging ~15 mA. On a 500 mAh cell that's roughly 33 hours, not the weeks the bistable display promises. Animation spends the entire power advantage of e-paper to buy 3 frames per second.

So when is it worth it?

When the animation is infrequent: a fish that moves every few seconds, a weather scene that redraws once a minute, a butterfly that flutters only when someone taps. Slow motion plus dirty rectangles keeps the average draw in the microamps. Continuous 20 fps animation is possible — people have run Bad Apple at 20 fps on raw-drive panels — but you're no longer building a low-power device, you're building a very slow screen.
